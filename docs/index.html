Why this matters, in plain words

When the motor surges, it drags the whole supply down for a moment — including the small voltage feeding the contactor's own coil. If that dips too far, the electromagnet lets go. The switch chatters or drops out, the motor stops, and the line halts for a reason nobody can see afterwards.

It is easy to miss, because everything looks perfectly normal a second later. That is the point of measuring against a written limit instead of watching a graph: the tool checks the exact two seconds that matter and writes down what it found.

This page simulates the data. The real tool reads it from a PLC over OPC UA, a standard industrial protocol — and a browser cannot speak that, which is exactly why this is a simulation.
